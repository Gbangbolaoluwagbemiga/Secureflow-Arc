SecureFlow Pitch Deck01

Somebody always goes first

Freelancing asks two strangers to trust each other with money, and neither has a reason to.

The freelancer's risk

Delivers the work, then waits. For a client who has gone quiet, who now says the brief meant something else, or who simply never pays.

The client's risk

Pays up front and the work may never arrive, or arrives as something they cannot use.

SecureFlow Pitch Deck03

We remove the middleman rather than replacing it

Money sits in a contract

Not in a company's bank account. No operator key can move a user's funds. Not ours.

Funded before it is visible

An unfunded job never appears on the board, so an application is never speculative work.

Paid per milestone

Payment releases against work the client actually accepted, not on a promise.

Disputes go to a human

An arbiter who can only choose between the two parties, and can never pay themselves.

SecureFlow Pitch Deck06

When it goes wrong

An arbiter rules, and the arbiter can only choose between the two parties. They can never pay themselves.

Multi-arbiter consensus

One to five arbiters with quorum-based voting, so no single party decides.

On-chain evidence

Both sides submit evidence against the disputed milestone. It is part of the record.

Time-limited windows

A dispute cannot hang open indefinitely and strand a freelancer's payment.

Proven, not theoretical

Four disputes have run this path end to end on Arc.

SecureFlow Pitch Deck07

Money nobody earned comes back

A job that attracts no suitable freelancer refunds automatically when its deadline passes.

Cancelling is not free. The penalty scales with how often you cancel and how many people already applied, capped at 30 percent — because cancelling a job people have written applications for wastes their time, and that should cost something.

Both guardrails are enforced by the contract, not by policy we could quietly change.

SecureFlow Pitch Deck08

Built for people who have never held a wallet

Sign in with Google or Telegram and a Circle MPC wallet is provisioned, gas included. Apply, deliver and get paid without the word "wallet" ever appearing.

The key is never assembled

It exists only as split shares. There is no export and no reveal — not for them, not for us.

We never hold their money

On approval the contract pays the worker's wallet directly. We sign instructions, we are not a custodian.

The exit is a withdrawal

To an address they control. Their money is fully theirs and fully extractable.

Or bring your own wallet

Crypto-native freelancers connect directly and are treated identically.

SecureFlow Pitch Deck11

Productive escrow

Escrow normally sits dead for weeks. Locked funds can be put to work while they wait.

Principal is never at risk of delay

Redeemable at face value, instantly, always. A yield failure can never block a payout or a dispute.

Ceiling and circuit breaker

Only a bounded share is ever deployed, and it stops automatically if the venue misbehaves.

The share is a term, not a setting

Fixed the day the job is posted, so it cannot be switched off after a freelancer accepts on the strength of it.

Venue-agnostic by design

A pluggable adapter, so the venue can change without touching escrow logic.
